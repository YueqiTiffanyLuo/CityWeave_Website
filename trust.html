AI clarifies. People decide.

When people report a problem to a public service, they should be able to see what the system understood and what happens to what they share.

What the AI does.

It observes, clarifies, records and suggests a route. If it isn't sure, it asks.

What it doesn't do.

It doesn't judge, enforce or decide how to fix a problem. Those decisions stay with your team.

What people share stays in their control.

Confirmed first

Nothing is sent until the person reporting has reviewed it and can correct what the system understood.

Never used for training

Voice and photos are never used to train AI. Sharing them with a team as evidence is optional.

Privacy in photos

Photos can be processed to protect the privacy of people caught in the frame, including their faces.

Only what's needed

We aim to collect only what a report needs and keep it only as long as it's useful.

Many ways in

Voice-first reporting helps people who find forms hard to use, in the language they speak.

Beside existing channels

Phone lines, apps and websites keep running. CityWeave adds a choice and takes nothing away.

What is DTPR?

DTPR stands for Digital Trust for Places and Routines. Think of it as a nutrition label for technology.

It's an open-source standard made up of simple icons and plain definitions. Together they explain what a system collects, why it collects it, who is accountable, and what rights you have.

It was developed with input from residents, public leaders and technologists, and is stewarded by Helpful Places. Boston and Washington, D.C. are among the places that have piloted it.

How it builds trust

People can see the system

Most digital systems are invisible. DTPR shows what's happening in terms anyone can follow.

The language is shared

The same icons mean the same thing from one place to the next, so people don't have to relearn them.

Questions get easier

When residents, staff and leaders can see the same explanation, they can have a better conversation about it.

How a report looks through DTPR.

311 Live uses DTPR icons and language to explain each step, so people can tell what was collected, what the system inferred and what still needs their approval.

  1. What you shareYour voice, photo and location.
  2. How it's understoodThe AI works out the issue and its impact from the conversation.
  3. What it suggestsA team to handle it. A suggestion only.
  4. What you controlCorrect anything before it's sent.
  5. Who reviews itPeople on the receiving team.
